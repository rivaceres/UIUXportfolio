Agrilakal –
from the farm, straight to the buyer

A farmer-first marketplace. It connects Philippine growers, buyers, distributors and the government, from harvest listing to verified delivery and payment. The name joins agrikultura (farming) and kalakal (goods, trade).

Problem Statement

Filipino smallholder farmers often can’t find reliable buyers before harvest. Produce goes unsold or sells at low farmgate prices. Institutional buyers struggle to find supply and coordinate purchases across locations. Price information is uneven, logistics are fragmented, and there is no trusted record of what was agreed, delivered or paid.

How might we give verified farmers earlier visibility to buyers, fair reference-based pricing and coordinated delivery, in a flow simple enough for low-bandwidth phones and six local languages, so more of every harvest is sold and paid for?

Target Users

Primary Users

Farmers - want to get verified, list harvests before picking, compare offers and get paid. They need simple step-by-step flows in their own language, low-bandwidth support, and LGU help when needed.

Buyers and wholesalers want to find crops by region, volume and date, and to negotiate and manage deliveries. They need verified sellers, a clear record of agreed terms, and a way to raise quality claims.

Distributors - want predictable jobs within their area and capacity, with clear pickup details and a way to report breakdowns.

Secondary Users

DA National and Regional Administrators- handle verification, disputes and reference prices.

LGU Agriculture Officers - run local identity checks and assisted onboarding.

Research & Key Insights

I didn’t run primary user research for this project. I based the problem, users and flows on secondary research: published reports and articles on Philippine smallholder farming, farmgate pricing, post-harvest loss and agricultural logistics. Five root causes stood out:

  1. Unsold harvests. Supply becomes visible to buyers too late, often only at or after harvest.
  2. Low farmgate prices. Farmers have weak negotiating power and few market channels.
  3. Information asymmetry. There is no shared, approved price reference, so middlemen hold the market information.
  4. Fragmented logistics. Transport is arranged ad hoc, and delivery status relies on trust, not evidence.
  5. Hard-to-find supply. Buyers can’t reliably locate volume across provinces.

Goal

Farmers sell more of each harvest, earlier, at prices aligned to the regional reference, and know when they’ve been paid.

Buyers find verified supply fast, lock terms in one structured offer, and receive what they ordered on time.

Distributors get jobs that fit their area and capacity, with evidence that settles every handover.

DA and LGU onboard and assist farmers safely, with every sensitive action scoped and auditable.

Final Designs

I designed 96 screens across four roles and three breakpoints, each shaped around one main job. Farmers can list a harvest, answer an offer and see when they’ll be paid. Buyers can turn a need into a purchase request, accept an offer and record payment. Distributors get jobs automatically, prove every pickup and drop-off, and can report a problem without being penalised. DA and LGU officials can see prices, volumes and disputes across their area.

Impact

This project produced a complete design package for Agrilakal: an information architecture, a user flow, wireframes, a design guide, a log of key design decisions, and high-fidelity screens for every role. The impact is still expected rather than measured. I see Agrilakal working best as a government-backed platform, run with the Department of Agriculture and LGUs. It could give smallholders a fairer route to market, publish reference prices people can trust, and cut unsold and wasted harvests. It would also give officials live data on supply, prices and disputes, so they can plan programs and respond faster. To show whether it works, I would track sell-through rate as the north star, alongside time to first qualified offer, on-time fulfilment, payment completion and dispute incidence, with targets set after a pilot baseline.
